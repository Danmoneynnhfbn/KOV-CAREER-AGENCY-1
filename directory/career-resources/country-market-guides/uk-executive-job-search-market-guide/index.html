UK Executive Job-Search Market Research Guide

The UK Office for National Statistics (ONS) publishes labour-market measures drawn from different sources and time periods. These indicators help describe national and regional conditions, but aggregate employment, vacancy, or earnings data do not establish demand for a particular CEO, CFO, or other executive role.

Separate the indicators before drawing conclusions

ONS labour-market publications include measures such as Labour Force Survey employment, unemployment, and economic inactivity rates; Pay As You Earn Real Time Information (PAYE RTI) payrolled employee estimates; vacancy estimates; and average weekly earnings. Each series has its own population, reference period, geography, source, and revision process.

ONS also publishes monthly summary data and separate RTI tables with geographic breakdowns. Check whether a figure refers to the United Kingdom, Great Britain, a devolved nation, or a more local area; do not treat these geographies as interchangeable.

Apply the data to an executive search carefully

  1. Set the question: Define the role remit, industry, ownership context, seniority, and location you are researching.
  2. Select a measure: Decide whether the question concerns employment, vacancies, payroll employment, earnings, or a different outcome. These measures are not substitutes for one another.
  3. Record the period and area: Note the reference period, release date, and geography. If the role is local, national data alone may not answer the question.
  4. Review quality notes: Check whether an estimate is provisional or subject to revision and note the data source and method stated by ONS.
  5. Keep executive evidence separate: Use employer disclosures, role descriptions, and verified hiring information for organization-specific questions. Do not convert an aggregate vacancy series into a count of executive openings.
  6. Refresh before a decision: Reopen the current ONS publication and linked dataset; do not rely on an archived figure without its date and context.

Create a concise UK market brief

For each target role, record the role hypothesis, source and dataset, measure, reference period, geography, release date, quality notes, and what the figure cannot establish. Add verified employer and sector information separately. If a data series cannot be mapped confidently to the role, label that evidence gap rather than presenting the measure as executive-market demand.

Scope and limitations

  • This guide covers UK labour-market indicators and source interpretation only. It is not employment-law, immigration, or compensation advice.
  • Aggregate employment, vacancies, or earnings measures do not establish executive-specific hiring, pay, or appointment outcomes.
  • ONS measures may cover different populations, periods, and geographies and may be revised; use the current source notes.
  • Direct UK-localized SERP, first-party demand, overlap, and KOV serviceability remain unverified. This staging page is not approved for publication.
