Japan Executive Job-Search Market Research Guide

Japan's Statistics Bureau conducts a monthly Labour Force Survey to describe employment and unemployment among households usually residing in Japan. The survey publishes a Basic Tabulation monthly and a Detailed Tabulation quarterly. For a senior-role search, knowing which tabulation and statistical series you are reading matters: national labour-force indicators do not identify demand for a particular employer or executive title.

Choose the tabulation that matches the question

The Basic Tabulation includes labour-force status, employment and employee counts with breakdowns such as industry, occupation and employment type, as well as hours worked and unemployment measures. The quarterly Detailed Tabulation covers additional topics, including job-change history, duration of unemployment and people wishing to work. Use the release notes and table definitions to confirm the period, population, classification and whether a measure is monthly or quarterly.

The Statistics Bureau directs users to historical data and the e-Stat portal for long-term datasets; it states that the monthly page no longer provides certain Excel-format summary data from October 2025 onward. A market brief should therefore record the actual table or database used, not only a high-level release page.

Check comparability before reading a trend

Seasonally adjusted series are revised. The Statistics Bureau announced a 2026 RegARIMA model revision, applied from the January 2026 results, with retroactive revisions to seasonally adjusted data. It also began providing occupation-related results using the 14th revision of the Japan Standard Industrial Classification from January 2026. A time series that crosses a seasonal-adjustment or classification change requires a check of the relevant notes and tables before drawing a conclusion.

Do not compare values from different series, periods or industrial classifications as if they were unchanged measurements. Retain the release date and the exact table definition with every observation used in a market hypothesis.

Prepare a Japan market-research brief

  1. Write down the target role's actual remit, function, industry and location; do not map a title to a statistical category without checking its definition.
  2. Select Basic or Detailed Tabulation based on the question and whether the relevant indicator is available monthly or quarterly.
  3. Record table ID, reference period, release date, geography, occupation or industry classification and original or seasonally adjusted status.
  4. Check the Statistics Bureau's revision announcements and e-Stat historical table metadata before comparing periods.
  5. Separate official labour-force indicators from employer disclosures, active job postings and recruiter conversations; they measure different things.
  6. State the limit: broad survey conditions do not establish executive vacancies, executive pay or an individual's appointment likelihood.

Scope and limitations

This guide is about interpreting Japan's official Labour Force Survey for a Japan-focused search. It is not immigration, employment-law, licensing or financial advice. The cited survey does not provide an executive search census or a forecast of an individual's job prospects. Japan-localized SERP research, first-party demand, overlap review and KOV serviceability remain open; the route is provisional.
