UK Civil Service Senior Role Success Profiles: Evidence Guide

The Civil Service uses Success Profiles to describe what applicants may need to demonstrate. The profile can draw on five elements: behaviours, strengths, ability, experience and technical requirements. The elements used vary by role. For senior roles, the official experience guidance says relevant experience is often needed, but the vacancy notice remains the authority on what will be assessed and how.

Start with the vacancy, not a generic profile

  1. Record each element and criterion named in the vacancy, along with the assessment method and any stated word or format limits.
  2. Separate essential requirements from desirable ones and preserve the advert's wording in a private preparation matrix.
  3. Check whether the process asks for a CV, application evidence, an interview, another assessment, or a combination. Do not assume one stage or method applies to every Civil Service appointment.
  4. Use the contact listed in the advert to clarify process questions. Do not infer unstated criteria from another department's campaign.

Show relevant experience through evidence

The official Success Profiles experience guidance describes experience in terms of relevant knowledge, mastery and achievement. It says assessment may consider what a candidate achieved, relevant subject knowledge and how they performed, rather than simply how long they served. For a senior application, translate broad responsibility into a specific work episode that makes your decisions, contribution and result understandable.

For each piece of evidence, prepare notes on the context, your remit, the decision or problem, the actions you personally took, the result, and what the evidence can substantiate. Keep the context concise. Explain why you chose your approach, how you handled constraints or competing interests, and which part of the outcome was yours versus the team's.

The official guidance lists an application form, CV and interview as possible ways to assess experience. It also says experience may be assessed alongside other Success Profile elements. Treat these as possible methods, not a guaranteed sequence: follow the vacancy's actual instructions.

Build an evidence-to-criterion matrix

Use a private working table to identify gaps before drafting. One piece of evidence may support more than one requirement, but tailor the explanation to the criterion being assessed.

Draft clearly without overstating

  • Distinguish your own actions from collective work, and do not claim a result you cannot support.
  • Use specific, relevant outcomes where you can verify them; omit numbers that cannot be checked or disclosed.
  • Explain the reasoning and choices behind an action, rather than relying on senior titles or broad portfolio descriptions.
  • Respect confidentiality and remove personal, protected or commercially sensitive details from your evidence.
  • Do not invent experience to match a criterion. If your evidence is adjacent rather than direct, say what transfers and what does not.

Scope and limitations

The Success Profiles framework does not mean every role tests all five elements. It does not establish a universal interview format, scoring threshold or guaranteed selection outcome. The official guidance says the vacancy should identify the relevant elements and assessment methods. This page is general preparation support, not recruitment, eligibility or legal advice.
