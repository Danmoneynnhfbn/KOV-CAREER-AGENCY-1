U.S. Federal Executive Pay Tables: Career Comparison Guide

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) publishes Executive and Senior Level employee pay-table pages by year. Its 2026 index lists several different table files. Before using a figure in career planning, identify the employee's actual pay system and the table that applies; the index alone does not determine an individual's compensation or eligibility.

Start with the official table index

OPM's 2026 Executive Senior Level page links to separate tables, including files labeled EX, ES, SLST, ALJ, AAJ, and BCA, with additional location-related table variants. Those labels point to different table files; do not assume one table applies to every federal executive, agency, or role.

Use the current OPM page and the employing agency's human-resources office to confirm the applicable pay system, table, year, and any individual terms. Do not rely on an old saved copy or infer a table from the word “executive” in a job title.

Build a careful career comparison

  1. Confirm the baseline: Record the official pay system, applicable table, year, and the compensation components actually shown for the role.
  2. Separate cash from other terms: List base pay separately from benefits, leave, retirement arrangements, incentives, or other offer terms. Verify each component with the responsible employer; this guide does not calculate their value.
  3. Check role scope: Compare responsibilities, decision authority, reporting relationships, location, and expected outcomes alongside compensation.
  4. Use like-for-like units: Confirm the period, currency, location, and treatment of any variable or deferred amounts before comparing figures.
  5. Keep the conclusion bounded: An OPM table describes the relevant federal pay schedule. It does not show private-sector market rates, an individual's likely offer, or what a role should pay.

Questions to resolve with the employer

  • Which pay system and table apply to the federal position, and which published year is current?
  • Which components are included in the figure being discussed, and which are separate?
  • For a non-federal offer, which terms are guaranteed, conditional, deferred, or governed by separate plan documents?
  • Which qualified tax, legal, or financial adviser should review questions outside career-planning scope?

Scope and limitations

  • The OPM source is a U.S. federal pay-table index and is not a private-sector salary survey.
  • This page intentionally publishes no salary figures, benchmarks, compensation forecasts, or role-equivalence ratios.
  • Compensation systems and terms vary by employer, role, location, and year; verify details directly with the relevant employer.
  • Direct candidate demand, competitor results, overlap, and KOV serviceability remain unverified. This staging page is not approved for publication.
