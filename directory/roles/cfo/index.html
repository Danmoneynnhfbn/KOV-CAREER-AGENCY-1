Chief Financial Officer (CFO): Role, Readiness and Career Positioning

A career guide for finance leaders pursuing CFO roles, with emphasis on financial stewardship, capital decisions, controls, investor confidence and strategic partnership.

Home / Executive roles / Chief Financial Officer (CFO)

What the role is accountable for

The CFO leads financial stewardship and helps the organisation make disciplined decisions. The remit commonly spans financial planning and analysis, accounting, treasury, tax, controls, capital structure, investor or lender relations and financial risk. Scope varies by company maturity, ownership and regulatory environment.

Typical responsibilities

Provide reliable financial reporting; translate performance into forward-looking insight; lead budgeting and forecasting; protect liquidity and financial controls; evaluate investments and financing options; support pricing, portfolio and operating decisions; and communicate financial performance to the board and capital providers.

What strong candidates need to demonstrate

Show the scale and complexity of the finance environment you have led: revenue or asset scale where disclosable, geographic footprint, team size, systems, audit environment and stakeholder groups. Strong evidence includes improved cash conversion, forecast reliability, margin discipline, successful financing, integration, control remediation, cost transformation or better capital allocation. Quantify only what can be substantiated.

Core leadership capabilities

Accounting and reporting discipline; commercial and operational finance; treasury and liquidity management; capital allocation; risk and internal controls; financial systems and data; investor communication; and the ability to challenge assumptions while remaining a constructive business partner.

Career pathways and readiness

Typical pathways include divisional CFO, regional CFO, finance director, group controller, treasurer or VP Finance. A move from a specialist finance track is stronger when supported by evidence of broad business partnership, people leadership, enterprise risk ownership and exposure to board-level decisions.

How to position your experience

Make the size and breadth of your remit clear. Distinguish statutory reporting ownership from management reporting, and direct team leadership from matrix influence. Prioritise decisions and outcomes over lists of finance processes. Explain the systems, controls and operating changes you led, while respecting confidentiality and accounting disclosure rules.

Interview preparation

Prepare to discuss a forecast miss, a liquidity or capital decision, a control weakness, a major investment case, a difficult audit or lender conversation and a time you challenged a commercial plan. Explain assumptions, alternatives, governance and lessons learned.

Questions to clarify before pursuing a role

  • What outcomes does the board or hiring organisation expect in the first year?
  • Which decisions, budgets, teams and risks sit directly within the role?
  • How is success measured, and what constraints or inherited challenges will shape delivery?
  • Which stakeholders have formal decision rights, and how does the role work with them?
