Chief Operating Officer (COO): Role, Readiness and Career Positioning

A career guide for operations leaders pursuing COO roles, covering operating models, execution, productivity, service quality, transformation and cross-functional leadership.

Home / Executive roles / Chief Operating Officer (COO)

What the role is accountable for

The COO turns strategic priorities into reliable operating performance. The remit may include operations, service delivery, supply chain, customer operations, process improvement, transformation or business-unit execution. Some COOs act as the CEO’s operational partner; others own a defined operating portfolio. The actual decision rights must be understood.

Typical responsibilities

Translate strategy into operating plans; align functions around performance priorities; establish management rhythms and escalation; improve capacity, quality and productivity; lead cross-functional change; and ensure operational risks and dependencies are visible to leadership.

What strong candidates need to demonstrate

Show results across the operating system: service levels, quality, throughput, unit cost, customer experience, safety, working capital, delivery reliability or transformation adoption. Explain the scale, baseline, time period and trade-offs. Do not present a local process improvement as an enterprise-wide result.

Core leadership capabilities

Operating-model design; performance management; process and systems thinking; financial and commercial judgment; cross-functional leadership; transformation delivery; customer and workforce insight; risk management; and disciplined communication.

Career pathways and readiness

Potential pathways include divisional COO, operations director, regional managing director, supply-chain leader, service-delivery executive or business-unit president. Candidates from a single function should demonstrate broad operating responsibility and the ability to lead peers across functions.

How to position your experience

Define the operating footprint: sites, regions, service lines, customers, workforce, suppliers and budget or P&L scope where appropriate. Lead with improvements in outcomes, not only projects completed. Clarify whether you owned the operation or led a programme within it.

Interview preparation

Prepare a performance recovery, a capacity or cost decision, a failed transformation, a customer-impacting incident, a cross-functional conflict and an example of building a stronger management cadence. Explain how you balanced short-term delivery with long-term capability.

Questions to clarify before pursuing a role

  • What outcomes does the board or hiring organisation expect in the first year?
  • Which decisions, budgets, teams and risks sit directly within the role?
  • How is success measured, and what constraints or inherited challenges will shape delivery?
  • Which stakeholders have formal decision rights, and how does the role work with them?
