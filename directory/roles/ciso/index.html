Chief Information Security Officer (CISO): Role, Readiness and Career Positioning

A career guide for security leaders pursuing CISO roles, covering cyber-risk governance, resilience, security strategy, incident leadership and executive communication.

Home / Executive roles / Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)

What the role is accountable for

The CISO leads or coordinates the organisation’s information-security strategy and cyber-risk programme. The role often spans security governance, risk assessment, incident readiness, identity, security architecture, third-party risk, awareness and regulatory engagement. Reporting lines and authority vary; clarify whether the mandate is advisory, operational or both.

Typical responsibilities

Translate business exposure into a prioritised security roadmap; establish risk ownership and escalation; oversee security controls and assurance; prepare for and coordinate incident response; assess third-party exposure; and communicate residual risk and investment choices to executives and the board.

What strong candidates need to demonstrate

Evidence should demonstrate risk reduction and organisational resilience, not tool counts alone. Examples include improved control coverage, shorter detection or recovery times, remediation of material findings, tested incident exercises, third-party risk improvements and clearer risk acceptance. Avoid disclosing exploitable technical details or confidential incident information.

Core leadership capabilities

Cyber-risk governance; security architecture and operations; incident command; identity and access management; third-party risk; assurance and audit; security culture; executive communication; and the judgment to prioritise controls according to business exposure.

Career pathways and readiness

Potential pathways include security operations leader, head of information security, security architecture leader, technology risk executive or regional CISO. A technical specialist moving into a CISO role should show governance, business alignment, crisis communication, budget decisions and influence beyond the security team.

How to position your experience

Describe the environment at an appropriate level: sectors, business scale, geographic footprint, regulated context where verified and team scope. Highlight decisions, governance maturity and resilience outcomes. Do not publish sensitive vulnerabilities, customer names, incident details or claims of certification that you cannot substantiate.

Interview preparation

Prepare for a material incident scenario, a disagreement over risk acceptance, a board briefing, a constrained security budget, a third-party compromise and a control programme that needed redesign. Explain decision rights, communications, evidence preservation and lessons learned.

Questions to clarify before pursuing a role

  • What outcomes does the board or hiring organisation expect in the first year?
  • Which decisions, budgets, teams and risks sit directly within the role?
  • How is success measured, and what constraints or inherited challenges will shape delivery?
  • Which stakeholders have formal decision rights, and how does the role work with them?
