Board CV and Director Biography: Presenting Governance-Relevant Experience

A board-facing profile should help its intended reader understand the experience a professional can substantiate and how that experience relates to a particular board context. The purpose of this guide is to help executives review evidence and make careful choices—not to prescribe a format that applies to every country, organization, or appointment process.

Start with the document’s purpose

Before editing, identify who requested the material, what decision it will support, the requested format and length, and the jurisdiction or governance context. If instructions are available, follow them. If they are unclear, ask the requesting organization or its adviser rather than assuming a single accepted format.

A board CV is typically a fuller record for review; a director biography is a shorter profile for an introduction or publication. Those labels and conventions vary. Confirm the use and audience instead of treating either label as a fixed standard.

Build an evidence map before writing

Use this as a prompt list, not a checklist of mandatory qualifications. Include an area only when it is relevant to the specific opportunity and supported by accurate, shareable evidence.

Questions to test whether a board-facing claim is clear and supportable
Evidence area Questions to ask What to verify
Governance and oversight What oversight, committee, advisory, or governance responsibilities did you actually hold? Role, dates, decision rights, and whether the responsibility was individual or collective.
Strategy and execution Which strategic decisions or significant changes did you help shape or deliver? Your specific contribution, context, timeframe, and any outcome you can document.
Risk, controls, and stewardship What relevant experience can you explain without overstating authority or expertise? The scope of your responsibility and the evidence permitted for disclosure.
Stakeholders and sectors Which stakeholder settings or operating contexts are relevant to the target organization? Specific experience and terminology appropriate to the organization and jurisdiction.
Results and scale Are figures or outcome statements necessary to explain the work? Source, period, attribution, confidentiality, and whether the figure fairly represents your role.

Organize the material for the reader

There is no single structure established by the sources cited here. A practical draft can include the following elements when requested and relevant:

  1. A concise opening that identifies the type of board context being considered and the professional’s verified experience.
  2. Selected governance-relevant evidence, with the professional’s own contribution distinguished from the work of a wider team or board.
  3. Career and appointment history with accurate titles, organizations, and dates.
  4. Relevant qualifications, affiliations, or current appointments, checked for accuracy and permission to disclose.
  5. A short biography derived from the same verified facts, adapted to the requested audience and word limit.

This is an editing aid, not a required sequence. Follow the organization’s instructions and seek appropriate professional advice where legal, regulatory, or disclosure obligations may apply.

UK governance context: the UK Corporate Governance Code

The UK Corporate Governance Code 2024 discusses board leadership and company purpose; division of responsibilities; composition, succession and evaluation; audit, risk and internal control; and remuneration. Those themes can help a reader understand one UK governance context, but the Code does not prescribe a board CV or director biography. Its scope and application are jurisdiction- and organization-dependent; do not treat it as a universal profile template or legal advice.

Run a claim-by-claim quality check

  • Confirm every title, date, qualification, appointment, and numerical statement against a reliable record.
  • Describe your own contribution precisely; do not attribute collective decisions or results solely to yourself.
  • Remove confidential, restricted, or personally identifying information unless disclosure is authorized and appropriate.
  • Check that the level of detail matches the recipient’s request and the rules that apply to the relevant organization.
  • Ask a qualified adviser about jurisdiction-specific duties, conflicts, regulatory disclosures, or legal questions.

Sources and limits

  • UK Corporate Governance Code 2024, Financial Reporting Council. Used only for the named UK governance topics; it does not define a CV or biography format.
  • Board and CEO Advisory, Spencer Stuart. Describes board and CEO search/advisory services; it is market context, not a document standard or evidence of appointment outcomes.
