Management Occupations: U.S. SOC Major Group 11-0000

This page explains one U.S. occupational classification and provides a limited route into related executive role information. It is not a global list of management jobs, a ranking of careers, or a statement that every occupation in the group is served by KOV.

What this classification means

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' 2018 Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) places Management Occupations in major group 11-0000. The SOC organizes occupations by work performed; it is not an industry classification. Management occupations may occur across industries, while each industry may employ people from many different occupational groups.

Classification helps provide a consistent language for describing work. It does not determine an individual's seniority, authority, qualifications, job title, compensation, or suitability for a role. Employers use titles differently, so the actual responsibilities and decision rights in a position matter more than a title match alone.

How to use an occupational classification in career research

  1. Start with the work. Identify the decisions, outcomes, functions, and scope of authority attached to the role rather than relying on title alone.
  2. Compare the employer context. The same title can carry different remit across organization size, ownership, sector, geography, and operating model.
  3. Translate evidence carefully. When describing your experience, connect responsibilities and results to the target role's needs. Do not assume that a SOC code or title establishes equivalence.
  4. Check the jurisdiction. U.S. SOC codes are not interchangeable with occupational frameworks used by other countries. Use the relevant local classification and labor-market sources when researching roles elsewhere.

Scope and limitations

  • This page refers specifically to the 2018 U.S. SOC. It does not present that system as an international standard.
  • Occupational classifications are statistical and descriptive tools; they do not prescribe how an employer must title or organize a job.
  • O*NET's reported job titles are not an exhaustive list or an individual eligibility test.
  • KOV does not provide legal, immigration, licensing, or government classification decisions through this resource.
  • Search demand, rankings, and outcomes are not claimed. Candidate-specific competitor and search-result analysis remains part of the development review.
