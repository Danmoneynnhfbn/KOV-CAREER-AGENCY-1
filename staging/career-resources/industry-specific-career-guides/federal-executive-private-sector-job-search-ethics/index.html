Federal Executives: Ethics Rules for a Private-Sector Job Search

What this guide is designed to help examine

This guide helps current U.S. federal employees identify when a private-sector job-search contact may call for agency ethics review and prepare questions for their own ethics office. It is not legal advice or a general government-career overview.

Intended audience: Current or recently departed senior U.S. federal employees considering private employers, contractors, or consulting.

Geographic scope: United States federal ethics context; agency-specific and circumstance-specific rules must be verified.

Plan a private-sector search with agency ethics guidance

Federal ethics obligations depend on the employee's agency, role, facts, and applicable law. The EPA's public guidance says that, for its employees, seeking employment can begin when an employee communicates, responds, or negotiates about possible employment. It lists sending a résumé or application, responding to an employer's approach other than to reject it, and using a headhunter when the prospective employer is known as situations that may count. EPA also describes recusal from matters affecting a prospective employer's financial interests once the employee begins seeking employment.

Those statements are an EPA-specific reference, not a legal conclusion for every federal employee. Before contacting a named employer, responding to a recruiter, submitting materials, or discussing a possible role, check the employee's own agency rules and speak with agency ethics counsel about the individual's circumstances. Do not rely on this page to determine whether recusal is required or whether a particular conversation is permitted.

A cautious preparation checklist

  1. Identify the agency ethics office and the current written guidance that applies to the employee's position.
  2. Before a conversation progresses, ask the ethics office how the agency treats the planned contact and what information it needs to assess the issue.
  3. Keep a factual record of relevant dates, employer identities, communications, and the agency advice received, following agency procedures.
  4. Ask whether any current matter, contract, regulation, or decision involving the prospective employer requires a change in work assignments or recusal, and follow official instructions.
  5. Before leaving government, ask separately about post-employment restrictions and required steps; do not assume that a job-search decision resolves them.

Questions to take to agency ethics counsel

  • What agency-specific rule governs seeking or negotiating employment in this situation?
  • At what point does a planned recruiter or employer contact require disclosure, consultation, or recusal?
  • Which matters should the employee stop participating in while the contact is active?
  • Are there restrictions after departure that apply to the employee's official work or prospective employer?
  • What written guidance or documentation should the employee retain?

The EPA source describes EPA procedures and cites federal rules in its own context. Other agencies may have additional processes, and the applicable legal analysis is fact-specific. This page does not provide legal advice, tell anyone to disregard official duties, or make a decision about an employee's eligibility to seek a role.

Primary reference: U.S. EPA, Ethics Rules for Employees Leaving Federal Service. Consult the employee's own agency ethics office for current, individualized direction.
