Federal Executive Core Qualifications: Career Evidence Guide

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) defines five Executive Core Qualifications (ECQs) for the Senior Executive Service (SES). Senior public-sector leaders exploring other executive roles can use the framework as a source of evidence to review, then translate their experience into the specific language and requirements of each target employer. The framework is not a private-sector standard, a personal eligibility determination, or a guarantee of appointment.

The five OPM qualification areas

OPM's current framework, updated 1 October 2025, organizes federal executive qualifications around:

  1. Commitment to the rule of law and the principles of the American founding.
  2. Driving efficiency.
  3. Merit and competence.
  4. Leading people.
  5. Achieving results.

OPM describes these qualifications as interdependent and states that they are used for SES entry, as well as by many federal departments and agencies in selection, performance management, and leadership development. That scope is federal; it should not be presented as a universal competency model.

Translate evidence without claiming equivalence

For each target role, make a record of what you did, the authority you held, the public purpose or operating objective, the people and resources involved, and the verifiable outcome. Then compare that evidence with the employer's actual role description. The goal is to make experience understandable—not to rename federal criteria as if the employer had adopted them.

  • Rule of law and public service: Describe governance, stewardship, and accountability in the context in which you worked. Do not equate public responsibilities with a private employer's fiduciary or legal duties.
  • Efficiency: Specify the resource or process decisions you owned, the constraints you navigated, and how the result was measured.
  • Merit and competence: Identify relevant expertise and decisions, while distinguishing technical contribution from enterprise-level authority.
  • Leading people: State the teams and relationships you led, the decisions you made, and how you handled accountability and change.
  • Achieving results: Connect goals to your actions and evidence. Separate personal contribution from collective or agency-wide results.

A practical evidence worksheet

  1. Choose one OPM qualification that is relevant to the specific target role.
  2. Write down the original mandate, your formal decision rights, and the result for which you were accountable.
  3. Identify evidence that can be substantiated and shared without disclosing protected, classified, personal, or confidential information.
  4. Map the evidence to the employer's stated requirements; mark gaps rather than treating a similar phrase as proof of equivalence.
  5. Have a reviewer challenge attribution, context, and clarity before using the material in a résumé, biography, or interview.

Scope and limitations

  • OPM's ECQs apply to a U.S. federal executive framework; this page does not describe the rules of other governments.
  • Private-sector employers set their own role criteria. A related competency label does not establish that the criteria are interchangeable.
  • This guide does not determine SES eligibility, provide federal hiring advice, or offer legal, ethics, or classified-information guidance.
  • Candidate search demand, direct competitors, full site overlap, and KOV serviceability remain unverified. This staging page is not approved for publication.
