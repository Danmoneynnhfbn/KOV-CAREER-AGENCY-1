Preparing for a U.S. Federal Structured Interview

The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) describes a structured interview as an assessment of job-related competencies through questions about past behavior, proposed behavior in hypothetical situations, or both. OPM also notes that candidates are generally asked predetermined questions in the same order and responses are evaluated against the same rating scale and standards. Confirm the process for a specific vacancy through its announcement or the hiring agency.

Prepare around the stated competencies

  1. Read the vacancy materials: List the stated competencies, responsibilities, and evaluation criteria. Do not assume that another agency or role uses the same process.
  2. Choose truthful evidence: Prepare concise accounts from your actual work that demonstrate relevant decisions and responsibilities. Distinguish your own actions from the work of the wider team.
  3. Structure the account: State the situation and your remit, explain the actions you took and why, then describe the result and what you learned. Use only outcomes you can substantiate.
  4. Practice hypothetical questions: State the assumptions you would clarify, the decision process you would use, the stakeholders and risks you would consider, and how you would check the result. Do not present a proposed action as a past achievement.
  5. Keep answers responsive: Address the question asked, make your individual role clear, and stop when the evidence and conclusion are complete. Ask for clarification when a question is unclear.

Use an evidence sheet, not a memorized script

For each competency in the vacancy, keep a private preparation note with the underlying work, your authority, the choices you made, the result, and any limits on what you can disclose. Prepare more than one truthful account where your experience permits, but do not assume any particular account will be requested.

If a question concerns a hypothetical situation, separate what you know from what you would first verify. Explain a reasoned course of action without claiming that the imagined circumstances occurred in your career.

Protect accuracy and confidentiality

  • Do not invent accomplishments, metrics, or responsibilities to fit a competency label.
  • Remove classified, personal, protected, or confidential details from preparation notes and answers.
  • When a result was shared, explain your contribution and avoid claiming the entire outcome as your own.
  • Do not treat the OPM description as a guarantee that a specific agency uses a structured interview or any particular scoring method.

Scope and limitations

  • OPM's source describes a U.S. public-sector assessment method; it does not establish how private-sector executive interviews are conducted.
  • This page does not provide a list of predicted questions, a scoring rubric, legal or ethics advice, or federal hiring eligibility guidance.
  • Direct SERP, first-party demand, overlap, and KOV serviceability remain unverified. This staging page is not approved for publication.
