Assessing a Non-Executive Director Opportunity: Governance Questions Before You Commit

A board invitation or expression of interest is a reason to clarify the context—not a substitute for it. Before deciding whether to proceed, ask for enough information to understand the organization, the role, the process, and the questions that require independent professional advice.

Start by defining the opportunity

Confirm the organization’s legal name, the type of entity, the jurisdiction, the appointing body, and whether the role is described as a director, non-executive director, chair, committee member, adviser, or another position. Titles can mean different things in different organizations and countries.

Ask for the current written role description and any process information. If the role is not clearly described, treat that as an open question to resolve rather than filling the gap with assumptions.

Questions to ask before deciding

Discussion prompts only; applicability and answers depend on the specific organization and jurisdiction.
Topic Questions to clarify Useful information to request
Role and remit What is the role expected to contribute? Which matters are within its stated remit, and which are not? Current role description, appointment process, reporting or governance structure, and committee information where applicable.
Organization context What should a prospective candidate understand about the organization’s purpose, strategy, ownership, and current priorities? Public filings or reports, governance materials the organization is willing to share, and an explanation of the current selection need.
Board and committee context How is the board or governing body organized? Are there particular skills or experience areas the process is seeking? Board composition information, committee remits where relevant, and the selection criteria used for this process.
Time and practical arrangements What activities, preparation, travel, or availability does the organization expect candidates to discuss? Meeting and preparation expectations, likely schedule, location or travel requirements, and who can clarify uncertainties.
Information and support What information is available to a prospective appointee, and what orientation or ongoing support is described? Induction information, access arrangements, key contacts, and a process for reviewing materials before making a decision.
Terms and specialist questions Which proposed terms, compensation details, conflicts, disclosure, or other issues need qualified review? Written draft terms and the appropriate legal, financial, tax, regulatory, or other professional adviser for the jurisdiction.

Compare the remit with your evidence and constraints

Once the organization has provided context, compare the stated needs with experience you can accurately describe. Separate direct evidence from adjacent experience, team achievements, and areas you would need to learn. Do not treat a sector label, past title, or brief conversation as proof of fit.

  • Which parts of the stated remit connect to work you have personally led, overseen, or supported?
  • Which parts of your experience can you substantiate without disclosing confidential information?
  • Which aspects of the organization or role remain unclear?
  • What practical constraints or competing commitments should you consider?
  • Which questions require an adviser or direct confirmation from the organization?

UK governance context: the UK Corporate Governance Code

The UK Corporate Governance Code 2024 is organized around board leadership and company purpose; division of responsibilities; composition, succession and evaluation; audit, risk and internal control; and remuneration. It offers context for questions about an organization that is within the Code’s scope. It does not answer every candidate’s questions, establish the rules for other jurisdictions, or replace advice on a particular appointment.

When to pause and seek clarification

  • The organization, appointing process, or role remit cannot be clearly identified.
  • Important statements are verbal only or conflict with materials supplied to you.
  • You are being asked to make a commitment before you have reviewed the relevant terms.
  • A legal, regulatory, financial, tax, conflict, or disclosure question is presented as something this guide can resolve.

These are prompts to ask further questions, not findings that an opportunity is sound or unsound.

Sources and boundaries

  • UK Corporate Governance Code 2024, Financial Reporting Council. A UK governance reference; it does not determine an individual opportunity’s terms or suitability.
  • Board and CEO Advisory, Spencer Stuart. Describes board and CEO advisory/search services; it is adjacent market context, not independent advice for a specific appointment.

Requirements and terminology vary by jurisdiction, entity, and appointment. Confirm details with the organization and qualified advisers. No appointment, compensation, or career outcome is claimed.
