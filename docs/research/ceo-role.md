# Research Sheet: Chief Executive Officer (CEO)

**Research date:** 22 October 2026  
**Status:** Source-backed drafting notes for `/roles/ceo/`; not public page copy.  
**Evidence rule:** Statements below are limited to pages and local source files actually opened for this task. “Source needed” is an explicit research gap, not permission to state a fact.

## Role definition and operating model

- The U.S. Department of Labor's O*NET OnLine profile for Chief Executives (11-1011.00; page opened 22 October 2026, current profile page) defines the work as formulating policy and providing overall direction within guidelines set by a board or similar governing body, and planning/directing/coordinating operations with subordinate executives and staff managers. Source: O*NET, “Chief Executives,” https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/11-1011.00.
- O*NET's task list includes coordinating budgets and financial activities; discussing issues with boards and staff; setting objectives and policy; analysing operations; selecting senior staff; negotiating agreements; coordinating departments; representing the organization externally; and serving on governing boards. The cited profile is U.S.-oriented and its occupation group includes titles beyond CEO; do not present it as a universal legal definition. Same source.
- O*NET's descriptor profile places decision-making, relationship-building, communication, directing teams, coaching, strategy, compliance judgment, negotiation, resource oversight, and staffing among the work activities. These describe the occupation profile, not a universal CEO assessment rubric. Source: O*NET OnLine, “Chief Executives: Details,” https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/11-1011.00.
- O*NET lists reported titles such as CEO, Executive Director, President and multiple chief-officer titles. This demonstrates title overlap in the U.S. data; it does not establish that these roles have identical remit or seniority. Source: O*NET “Chief Executives” page above.
- The UK government's director guidance says a director is responsible for running the company and required filings; a director remains responsible even if a professional is hired to help. The source describes UK company-director duties, not all CEO duties and not other jurisdictions. Source: GOV.UK, “Being a company director,” https://www.gov.uk/guidance/being-a-company-director (page opened 22 October 2026).
- The UK Corporate Governance Code 2024 distinguishes board leadership from executive leadership of the company's business; it says non-executive directors provide strategic guidance and hold management to account, and says the chair and CEO roles should not be held by the same individual within the Code's scope. This Code is UK-specific and applies on a comply-or-explain basis. Source: Financial Reporting Council, UK Corporate Governance Code 2024, issued 22 January 2024, https://www.frc.org.uk/docs/6709/html/ (opened 22 October 2026).

## How companies and boards hire

- CIPD's recruitment factsheet describes recruitment as work from defining the role through attracting applicants, managing application and selection, and making the appointment. It says effective resourcing addresses current and future workforce needs, rather than only an immediate vacancy. Source: CIPD, “Recruitment: An introduction,” published 28 May 2026, https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/recruitment-factsheet/ (page opened 22 October 2026; detailed text is membership-gated).
- O*NET documents CEO interactions with boards and the role's relationship to the governing body's guidelines. This supports describing board/owner alignment as a role-context question, not a universal hiring sequence. Source: O*NET OnLine, “Chief Executives,” above.
- AESC's accessible homepage warns about recruitment scams impersonating executive-search firms and legitimate opportunities and advises verifying sender and firm. It does not describe CEO sourcing or shortlist methods. Source: Association of Executive Search and Leadership Consultants, https://www.aesc.org/ (opened 22 October 2026).
- NACD describes CEO succession planning as a board responsibility and reports its 2019 governance survey findings: nearly three-quarters of responding directors cited maintaining the CEO pipeline as a leading challenge; about 80% reported recent long-term succession discussions; fewer than 19% reported comparing desired CEO competencies against future strategic needs. These are historical survey results, not current universal recruiter behavior. Source: National Association of Corporate Directors, “CEO Succession Planning,” Governance Challenges 2019, https://www.nacdonline.org/all-governance/governance-resources/governance-research/outlook-and-challenges/governance-challenges-2019-ceo-succession-planning/ (opened 22 October 2026).
- Spencer Stuart describes succession as an ongoing process and recommends deriving future-leader requirements from strategy, assessing internal candidates, benchmarking against external talent, and planning transitions. Source: Spencer Stuart, “Boardroom Best Practice — Chapter 5,” publication date not displayed, https://www.spencerstuart.com/research-and-insight/boardroom-best-practice-chapter-5 (opened 22 October 2026).
- Spencer Stuart describes the board as ultimately responsible for CEO succession planning and selection; it says some boards use a smaller committee for detailed assessment and benchmarking with full-board involvement at key points. This is advisory guidance, not a universal process. Source: Spencer Stuart, “How Boards Can Overcome the Most Common Succession Planning Obstacles,” publication date not displayed, https://www.spencerstuart.com/research-and-insight/how-boards-can-overcome-the-most-common-succession-planning-obstacles (opened 22 October 2026).
- Korn Ferry describes its own CEO search/succession work: it develops a strategy-linked Success Profile and describes internal assessments and external candidate identification/appraisal. This is firm-specific, not a standard followed by every recruiter. Source: Korn Ferry, “CEO Search & Succession,” publication date not displayed, https://www.kornferry.com/capabilities/board-ceo-services/ceo-search-succession (opened 22 October 2026).
- **Source needed:** typical search-firm sourcing channels, shortlist size, CEO search timelines, and universal candidate-consent/confidentiality practices. Do not assert these as universal facts in public copy.

## Recruiter/board screening: evidence categories supported for guidance

O*NET's role definition and tasks support presenting the following as **questions to prepare evidence for**, not as a claimed universal recruiter scorecard:

1. **Governance and remit:** What authority did the candidate hold, and what was set by a board or similar body?
2. **Enterprise direction:** What policy, objectives, strategy, or organizational direction did the candidate formulate or execute?
3. **Operating leadership:** What operations, functions, sites, or subordinate executives were within the actual remit?
4. **Financial stewardship:** What budget, investment, or operating decisions were owned or influenced?
5. **People leadership:** Which senior appointments, structures, teams, or development decisions were the candidate accountable for?
6. **Stakeholder work:** Which board, shareholder, workforce, customer, government, or external relationships did the role require?
7. **Risk, controls and compliance:** What standards or constraints did the candidate have to interpret and uphold?
8. **Verifiable outcomes:** What evidence supports claims? O*NET identifies reports, budgets, operations and results as work tasks, but does not prescribe a hiring evidence format.

**Source needed:** named executive-search firms' published methodology explaining their actual CEO screening criteria and endorsement process. Avoid claims such as “boards always hire X,” “CEOs are sourced only through networks,” or “a CEO must have prior CEO experience.”

## CEO-specific candidate-presentation risks and corrections

The risks below are writing/positioning recommendations derived from the documented title overlap and role tasks, not measured error prevalence:

- **Title without remit:** “CEO,” “president,” “executive director,” or “managing director” alone can obscure authority and scale. Add the reporting/governance context, functions, team, and decision scope without inventing metrics. (O*NET title sample and definition.)
- **Strategy without execution evidence:** Connect direction-setting to the operations, organizational decisions, and results that can be substantiated. (O*NET tasks.)
- **Impact with no personal contribution:** Separate individual decisions from collective board and executive-team outcomes; never imply sole credit where decisions were shared. (O*NET's board/staff coordination tasks.)
- **Confidentiality breach:** Do not include protected board materials, unpublished transactions, privileged advice, customer/personnel information, or confidential financial details in a CV, biography, portfolio, or recruiter outreach. **Source needed** for jurisdiction-specific confidentiality rules; this is a cautious document-handling recommendation, not legal advice.
- **Inflated or stale public profile:** Keep titles, dates, and claims consistent with verifiable history. Do not publicly announce a confidential search unless the candidate approves it.
- **Using one document for every audience:** A résumé/CV, board biography, and LinkedIn profile have different reading contexts. This is a KOV editorial recommendation, not a sourced recruiter requirement.
- **Applying as if every CEO vacancy had one hiring authority:** Identify whether the role is governed by a board, owner, public body, or another authority before directing an approach. The exact decision process is **source needed** for each target organization.
- **KOV document handling:** The public materials reviewed do not establish retention/deletion periods, technical security controls, or jurisdiction-specific privacy guarantees. **Source needed** before making any such claim; ask for an approved KOV policy.

## FAQ topics unique to the CEO role

1. **Does “CEO” mean the same thing as “president” or “executive director”?** O*NET reports overlapping titles in its U.S. occupation profile; the actual remit must be checked at the employer. (O*NET, current profile.)
2. **Does a CEO report to the board?** O*NET describes overall direction within guidelines set by a board or similar governing body; the actual reporting structure is employer-specific. (O*NET, current profile.)
3. **Can a chief functional officer be presented as a CEO candidate?** O*NET groups several chief-officer titles in the Chief Executives occupation profile, but does not state that every functional chief qualifies for CEO roles. Compare the candidate's actual organization-wide remit with the target job. (O*NET.)
4. **What should a CEO CV show first?** Use a concise, accurate remit and evidence of direction, operations, financial/people scope, and governance interfaces, reflecting O*NET's listed tasks. Do not invent a universal format rule. (O*NET.)
5. **Should a CEO publish a confidential search on LinkedIn?** No universal rule is supported by the opened sources. A candidate should control public disclosure; platform-specific or legal obligations are **source needed**.
6. **Can a CEO send board packs or internal dashboards to a recruiter?** The opened sources do not define permissions. Avoid sharing restricted material; obtain authorization and seek qualified advice where needed. **Source needed** for jurisdiction-specific law.
7. **Do CEO searches follow a fixed timeline?** No. The opened sources do not support a standard CEO-search duration.
8. **Does KOV guarantee a board interview or appointment?** No: KOV's existing public service content says it does not promise employment, interviews, hiring, or timelines because employers make hiring decisions. Source: current homepage `index.html`, opened locally 22 October 2026.
