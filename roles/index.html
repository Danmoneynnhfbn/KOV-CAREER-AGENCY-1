Make your next move about the work

A title is a starting point, not a complete explanation of the value you bring.

A strong career conversation begins with the decisions, responsibilities, and working conditions you want to take on. Consider the kind of organization, scope, mandate, and leadership relationships that make sense for your next step. Then connect that direction to evidence from your own experience rather than relying on a title to make the case.

KOV's candidate-side support can help organize this thinking into a practical positioning and search plan. Work is scoped with you, and you remain responsible for the decisions about which opportunities to pursue and what information may be shared.

  • Clarify the role and mandate you want before preparing application materials.
  • Separate responsibilities you owned from work shared with colleagues or a governing body.
  • Use evidence that you can explain and substantiate.
  • Set boundaries around location, working arrangements, confidentiality, and timing.

Documents that support your direction

Career materials should make it easier for a reader to understand your background and assess relevance to a specific opportunity. Start with the facts: roles, dates, scope, contributions, and outcomes you can support. Use clear language, explain context where needed, and avoid implying that you had authority or achieved results that belonged to a larger team.

KOV's individual career services include support for resumes and CVs, LinkedIn profiles, career documents, portfolios, and personal career websites. The appropriate deliverable depends on your objective and the work agreed with KOV. You review personal materials and retain control over whether and where they are used.

  • Confirm facts and figures before including them.
  • Distinguish personal contribution from collective outcomes.
  • Adapt emphasis to the opportunity without changing the underlying facts.
  • Keep sensitive employer, colleague, and client information out of public-facing material.

A generic example of clearer career writing

Before

Responsible for business improvement and team leadership.

After

Led a cross-functional improvement initiative, clarifying decision ownership and tracking agreed operational measures.

Keep outreach considered and controlled

Professional outreach should reflect an agreed purpose and an appropriate level of disclosure. Before any contact, discuss the audience, the information that may be shared, and how a response should be handled. KOV's service description refers to approved outreach and follow-up; the candidate remains involved in decisions about fit and next steps.

If confidentiality is important, explain the constraints early. Agree how to refer to your background, which details must remain private, and how to handle a conversation that moves faster than expected. Do not assume that a recipient will treat an unsolicited message as confidential.

Prepare for conversations and decisions

Interview preparation can help you present relevant examples, describe your decision-making, and ask questions about the mandate. Build a small set of accurate examples that show context, your role, the choices available, and what followed. Be ready to discuss what you learned and what you would approach differently.

If an opportunity reaches offer discussions, assess the role's responsibilities and terms against your priorities. KOV lists coaching and executive offer support among its services. The scope of any assistance is agreed with you; the employer determines its offer, and you decide whether to accept or negotiate.

Review the role description, choose relevant examples, prepare thoughtful questions, and decide which points need clarification.

Listen for the mandate, decision rights, expectations, and practical conditions. Ask for detail when an important point is unclear.

Compare the written terms and role scope with your priorities. Take time to understand open questions before making a decision.

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose the right role guide?

Start with the work and mandate you are considering, then review the available guide for relevant questions about positioning and career support. The current role-specific guide is for CEOs.

Do I need private career representation to improve my documents?

No. KOV also lists individual services for documents, profiles, positioning, portfolios, websites, and coaching. Discuss your needs to identify an appropriate scope.

Who decides whether an opportunity is right for me?

You do. KOV can provide agreed candidate-side support, while you decide which opportunities to pursue and how to respond.

Can KOV guarantee an interview or job offer?

No. Employers make hiring decisions, and KOV does not guarantee interviews, offers, employment, or timelines.

What should I prepare for an initial conversation?

Bring a concise outline of your goals, relevant experience, constraints, and the support you are considering. You do not need to send sensitive information before discussing how it will be handled.
